For the first three weeks of daycare, my daughter cried at drop-off. Not a whimper. The full thing — clinging to my leg, tears, the small devastated face pressed against the window as I walked to the car. I sat in the parking lot and cried too, twice. I was convinced I had made a terrible mistake and possibly ruined her for life.

The teachers kept telling me the same thing every daycare teacher tells every parent: "She stops crying two minutes after you leave." I did not believe them. I pictured her sobbing in a corner until pickup. Then one morning I came back because I'd forgotten her water bottle, and there she was, ten minutes after drop-off, happily smearing paint on a smock. The crying really was just for the goodbye.

That changed my whole approach. The crying wasn't about daycare. Daycare was fine. The crying was about the transition, and I was making the transition worse. My goodbyes had become a twenty-minute negotiation — one more hug, okay one more, do you want to bring teddy, let's wave at the fish tank, I'm really leaving now, okay one more hug. I was dragging out the hardest part of her morning because watching her cry made *me* feel better to linger. It didn't help her. It gave her twenty minutes of anticipating the separation instead of two.

So I made the goodbye short and boring. Same script every day: bag in the cubby, one hug, "I love you, I'll see you after nap time," hand her to the teacher, walk out. No sneaking off — that's the one thing everyone agrees on, and they're right; she caught me once and the trust damage lasted a week. But no lingering either. The first three days of the short goodbye were awful. Day four, she cried for about a minute. By week two, she was walking in ahead of me.

The second thing that helped: I gave the goodbye a job. She hands her bag to the teacher herself. It's a tiny responsibility, but it turned her from "person being abandoned" into "person with a task." Toddlers would rather be useful than sad.

What didn't work: promising pick-up treats. "If you're good, we'll get ice cream!" just taught her that daycare was a thing to endure for a reward, and on the days there was no ice cream she was furious. Also didn't work: me being cheerful-loud. "BYE SWEETIE HAVE THE BEST DAY!!!" while she cried just felt like I was mocking her grief. Quiet and warm beat loud and peppy.

The hardest truth: most of the drop-off pain was mine. She adjusted in about two weeks. I took closer to a month to stop feeling guilty in the parking lot. If you're in the crying phase right now — it is a phase. Make the goodbye boring, don't sneak, and trust the paint smock.
