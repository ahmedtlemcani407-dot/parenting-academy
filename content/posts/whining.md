It started when she was about three and a half. That voice. You know the one — it comes out of nowhere around 6pm, right when I'm trying to get dinner on the table, and it drills directly into the center of my skull.

"Muuuum, I want the blue cup, not the greeeen cuuup."
"My socks feel weeeird."
"Carry meeee."

It's not crying. Crying I can handle — crying means something's wrong and I go fix it. Whining is static. It's the sound of a small person who is tired and hungry and slightly out of sorts, broadcasting it at full volume with no off switch.

My first strategy was the classic: "I can't understand you when you whine. Use your normal voice." She stared at me, took a breath, and whined louder. So that was a flop.

My second strategy was ignoring it completely. Just not responding until the whining stopped. What actually happened: she escalated. The whine became a wail, the wail became full-body sobbing on the kitchen floor, and dinner burned. Ignoring a whining toddler is like ignoring a smoke alarm — technically possible, practically insane.

My third strategy was the worst one: I caved. One evening, fried after work, I just handed her the biscuit she was whining for to make it stop. It worked! For exactly one evening. The next day the whining doubled, because she'd learned the exchange rate: whine long enough, get biscuit. I'd trained her like a very small, very tired Pavlovian experiment.

What actually reduced it — not eliminated, reduced — was two things I do together now.

First, I name the feeling before I address the request. "You're frustrated because you're hungry and dinner's not ready." Half the time, she just needed to hear that I got it. The whining is often just a feeling wearing a costume.

Second, one calm redo. "Try that in your regular voice." If she does — even a rough attempt — I respond immediately and warmly. "Thank you! You'd like the blue cup. Here you go." The fast reward for the normal voice matters more than any correction of the whiny one.

I should also confess my lowest moment in all of this: one evening I mocked her. She was whining for juice and I did the whiny voice right back at her — "juuuice pleeease" — thinking it would hold up a mirror and she'd hear how silly it sounded. She burst into tears. Real, heartbroken tears, because as far as she knew, mum was being mean for no reason. I felt about two inches tall. We had a cuddle, I apologized properly, and I have never done it since. Mocking a three-year-old: zero out of ten, do not recommend.

It didn't happen overnight. For about a week she tested whether the new system was real. But the 6pm static has dropped by maybe seventy percent, and on the evenings it spikes, I now know it means she's overtired and needs dinner ten minutes earlier, not a lecture on tone of voice.
