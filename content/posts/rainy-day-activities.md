It rained for nine straight days last month. Nine. By day four my daughter was climbing the walls — literally, she tried to climb the hallway wall — and I was one more episode of the same cartoon away from losing it. So we tested everything. Here's what actually worked, ranked by how long it bought me.

**1. Couch cushion fort.** The undefeated champion. Every cushion off, blankets over the top, flashlight inside. She played in it for an hour and a half. An HOUR and a half.

**2. Painter's tape roads.** Tape roads all over the living room floor, cars driving between the "cities" (cushions). Forty minutes, and the tape came right off the wood.

**3. Sink play.** A step stool, the bathroom sink full of warm water, plastic cups and a turkey baster. She "washed" her toy animals for fifty minutes. I sat on the closed toilet and drank coffee. Bliss.

**4. Sticker scenes.** A roll of cheap dot stickers and big paper. She made what she called "a family" out of dots. Thirty-five minutes.

**5. Laundry basket boat.** Basket on a blanket, she sits in it, I drag her around the apartment while she yells "faster." Twenty-five minutes and my back hurt, but worth it.

**6. Playdough with real kitchen tools.** Garlic press, cookie cutters, a rolling pin. The garlic press was the star — she made "spaghetti" for twenty minutes straight.

**7. Flashlight shadow puppets.** Dark bedroom, flashlight, hands on the wall. She was more interested in the flashlight itself, but it still bought twenty minutes.

**8. Sorting the spice jars.** I know. But she loved lining them up by size and smelling each one. Fifteen quiet minutes. The cumin incident was survivable.

Now the flops, because honesty matters:

**9. Baking together.** Every blog says bake with your toddler. She ate raw flour, cried when I wouldn't let her crack the egg, and I finished the muffins alone while she watched cartoons. Never again until she's five.

**10. The fancy craft kit.** I bought a 20-euro "toddler art studio" box. She used it for six minutes, then asked for the cardboard box it came in. The box is now a garage for her toy cars. The kit is in a closet.

Honorable mention that didn't make the list because it's barely an activity: washing vegetables. I put her on the step stool at the kitchen sink with a colander of carrots and she washed each one like it was a tiny baby. Fifteen minutes of peace while I actually got dinner started. Sometimes the activity is just... letting them do the boring thing next to you.

The real lesson from nine days of rain: the best activities are the ones where the house already has the supplies and I don't have to perform. Water, tape, cushions, a flashlight. She doesn't need Pinterest. She needs me on the floor with her, slightly bored, fully there.
