I used to have very strong opinions about screen time. This was before I had kids, when it was easy. My plan was simple: no screens until age three, then very limited educational content only. It lasted about four months into actual parenthood.

The first time I handed my daughter a phone was in a doctor's waiting room. She was 18 months old, screaming, and I had a choice between my principles and the sanity of twelve strangers. I chose the strangers. And you know what? The world didn't end.

I'm not saying screens are great. I'm saying the guilt cycle around them was worse for me than the screens were for my kids. I spent a year feeling bad every time the TV went on, and that bad feeling didn't reduce anyone's screen time by a single minute. It just made me a grumpier parent.

So we made rules. Not aspirational rules, actual ones we've kept for over a year now:

One: no screens during meals. This is the one we never break. The table is where we talk, even if the talking is mostly about dinosaurs.

Two: no screens in the hour before bed. This one we break maybe once a month when life is chaos, and every time we do, bedtime is harder. So the rule keeps proving itself.

Three: we watch together when we can. This changed everything. When my daughter watches something alone, she zones out. When we watch together and I ask dumb questions about what's happening, it becomes something else entirely. She remembers more. She talks about it later. I'm not pretending Daniel Tiger is educational in some deep way, but co-watching at least makes it social.

Four: the parent puts the phone down too. This was the hard one. My kids don't care about my screen time rules for them if I'm scrolling at the playground. I started leaving my phone in the kitchen during the after-dinner play hour. I won't lie, the first week was rough. Now I barely notice.

What we stopped doing: timers that beep. Every timer app we tried ended in a meltdown when the thing went off mid-show. Instead we use natural stopping points. "This episode, then we're done." It works about 80% of the time, and the other 20% I just ride out the complaining. Complaining is allowed in our house. It's not an emergency.

Here's the part nobody told me: the amount of screen time matters less than what it's replacing. An hour of TV instead of an hour of me staring at my own phone while she plays alone? That's a wash, maybe better. An hour of TV instead of the park on a beautiful Saturday? That's different, and I feel it. So I stopped counting hours and started asking that question instead. It made the whole thing less miserable.

If you're feeling guilty about it right now, here's my honest take as someone who's been there: set two or three rules you can actually keep, put your own phone down during the important hours, and let the rest go. Your kids need a relaxed parent more than they need a screen-free one.
